# Roster — Cold Call Script (home-services owners)

**Call through Quo (901 number)** so a US caller ID shows, not +91. Voice calls are NOT
blocked by the A2P SMS registration — only texting is.

You impress these owners by: respecting their time, knowing their business, a sharp
risk-free offer — NOT hype. They're on jobs; be fast and real.

---

## 1. Opener (permission-based — earns the 20 seconds)
> "Hey, is this **[Owner]**? … Hey [Owner], my name's Nandan. I'll be straight with you —
> this is a cold call. Give me 20 seconds, and if it's not useful, hang up on me. Fair?"

Almost everyone says "ok, go." The honesty is the pattern-interrupt.

## 2. Hook (personalize + ask, don't pitch)
> "I looked you up — **[Business]**, you've been running it **[their signal: solo since
> 2014 / since '04 / just you and your wife]**, and it's clear you take a lot of the calls
> yourself. Quick question — when you're out on a job and a call comes in you can't grab,
> what happens to it right now?"

Let them answer. Voicemail / rings out / wife grabs it — whatever they say is the pain.

## 3. Pain + solution (concrete, calm)
> "Right. And the thing is, most people who hit voicemail just call the next guy — they
> don't leave a message. That's a job gone. I set up an assistant that instantly texts
> those missed callers back, answers them, gets the job details, and books it for you —
> so you stop handing those to the competition. You don't change how you work. It just
> catches what you'd miss."

## 4. Offer (risk-free — this is what closes)
> "Here's why I'm calling instead of emailing: I'll set the whole thing up myself, free,
> and run it two weeks on your line. You don't pay a dollar unless it actually books you
> real jobs. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing. Worth a try?"

## 5. Close (small next step)
> "Great — all I need is 10 minutes to grab your services, hours, and pricing so it sounds
> exactly like you. Better later today or tomorrow morning?"

---

## Objection handling
- **"How much?"** → "Free for two weeks, fully set up by me. After that you only pay per
  job it books — so it only ever costs you once it's already made you money."
- **"I already have an answering service / someone."** → "Love it — does it *text* the
  missed callers back in seconds and book them, or just take a message? Mine's free for
  two weeks, so worst case you compare. Worth a look?"
- **"I don't want robots talking to my customers."** → "Fair. It *texts* — short, normal
  texts, no robocalls. And for two weeks I watch every conversation myself so it sounds
  right. You'll see it before you trust it."
- **"I'm on a job / busy."** → "Totally — that's the whole point. 30 seconds: can I text
  you a link and call back tomorrow at [time]?" Then DO it.
- **"Where are you calling from?"** → Be honest: "I'm in India, building this for US
  home-services pros — that's why I set it all up for you, zero work on your end." Honesty
  builds trust; never dodge it.

## Voicemail (under 20 sec — most calls go here, that's fine)
> "Hey [Owner], it's Nandan — I help [trade] owners stop losing jobs to missed calls by
> texting customers back automatically. I'll set it up free for two weeks; you only pay if
> it books you work. Text me back at this number and I'll send details. Thanks [Owner]."
